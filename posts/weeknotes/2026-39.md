## OKR Daily Card is live

![OKR Daily Card desktop view, showing today's objective, daily actions and key results](/img/assets/2026/09/okr-daily-card-desktop.png)

The side project I've been poking at for a few weeks now has a real URL: **[okr.sijobling.com](https://okr.sijobling.com/)**, running on Railway.

It started as a single-user demo for tracking quarterly OKRs, a mission per objective, a few daily actions to tick off, key results they roll up to. Every tick asks "what did you actually do?" so the history is real evidence rather than a bare streak count. Over the last couple of weeks it's turned into something closer to a proper (if still very personal) app: magic-link sign-in instead of a password, a guided onboarding flow that turns your own answers into your own objectives and daily actions rather than my invented demo data, a weekly view with a "copy week summary" button for pasting into a check-in doc, and an MCP hook-up so Claude can check in on it directly. Ten stories in, all done. Nothing fancy, just useful.

## Two PETALS posts out this week

Both landed on the [PETALS blog](https://usepetals.com/blog) Thursday evening, back to back.

**[Is AI a friend, a foe, or just a really enthusiastic junior?](https://usepetals.com/blog/is-ai-a-friend-foe-or-just-an-enthusiastic-junior/)** starts from Atlassian's research that nearly half of knowledge workers say their AI knows about as much about their company as a brand new hire, and asks where that actually leaves AI in a people-first framework like PETALS. Short version: not a sixth petal, not a priority to optimise for its own sake, just an assistant that needs the same onboarding and context you'd give any new hire.

**[Conversational debt and the price two teams pay for silence](https://usepetals.com/blog/conversational-debt-and-the-price-two-teams-pay/)** takes Gustavo Razzetti's idea of conversational debt (every avoided crucial conversation compounds like an unpaid loan) and runs it through two hypothetical teams at different sizes to see exactly where psychological safety does and doesn't intervene.

No dev-time session this week though, everyone was busy. Still caught up async on a few feature and content updates between us. Fine by me, some weeks go like that on a side project.
